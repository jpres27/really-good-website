Why is this website like this?

by John Presley

2025-3-1

I spent some time writing a variety of more modern looking websites at first. There were animations and information density was low. In the end, I didn't care for them enough to keep trying to develop them and I already am a neophyte in website design so I couldn't get far without at least some passion. In the end, writing this directly into a raw HTML file is somehow more interesting to me. It might appear both gauche and unbearably primitive but there is something to be said for maintaining a relationship to a more protean state of affairs.

In a time before even jQuery, between the sinking of Atlantis and the rise of the sons of Aryas, it was an age undreamed of. Including what is now most unimaginable: delivering static documents. What is the difference between a world of enthusiasts serving their HTML recipes and journals and essays directly across the earth and maintaining a Wordpress blog or Substack? There are many; one would be that they are different forms of life and these forms each make possible different kinds of thinking. Different modes of thought obtaining is owed to differing relationships to the subtrate of their work.

The people who carved their thoughts into the rocks of bytes were freeholders. Like Orlando Bloom in Kingdom of Heaven, they tilled their own land and dug their own wells. For those who insist on the unequivocal value of pushing specialization to its limits, we can at least offer the consolation that most were not personally implementing HTML 1.0 in C on servers that lived on their bedroom floor. Though some were.

Nescire autem quid ante quam natus sis acciderit, id est semper esse puerum. How often do we see people proposing things they believe are new which actually possess quite reverend pedigrees? How common is it to see people revel in pseudo-intellectual posturing because they never witnessed actual greatness in thinking and true subtlety in understanding?

To put it in a way which appeals to contemporary tastes, "What I cannot build, I do not understand." I can't claim to understand databases if I can't build one. Ditto for forms of life. How many programmers all talk to each other on Discord, read Twitter, scroll Hacker News, and have roughly identical Youtube feeds? And the result is unsurprisingly the same handful of opinions on every issue being bandied as entirely novel and unassailably correct. And many of these same people cannot deviate from Bootstrap web design even when they're not using Bootstrap.

It is a small number of programmers who are interested in what programmers before them did, outside of John Carmack hero worship. It is an even smaller number of them who take any interest in what humanity did before their time. The great storehouse of accumulated human experience and wisdom, the forge wherein all of the disciplines they idolize were created, lay open before them and go utterly neglected.

It can't be helped. As for me, I'd like for my personal website to look like this and be built like this. I'd like to have some taste of what it was like in older times and how people then interacted with their computers and how they may have thought about things when they were building their Anime Web Turnpikes and RPGamers.
